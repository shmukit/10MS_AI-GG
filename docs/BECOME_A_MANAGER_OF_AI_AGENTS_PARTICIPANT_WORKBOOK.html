1.1 What is AI?

🧠 TASK 0.0 (~5 min) — What is AI? What to expect / not expect
Do: Follow the instructor. On this page (or a note), write one line you expect today and one you will not expect.
Done when: Both lines written. Mark roadmap 0.0 complete.

In plain words:
AI is software that can learn patterns from large amounts of data and then produce useful outputs — text, summaries, suggestions, classifications - without you writing every rule by hand.

Analogy:
Think of a very fast new junior who has read a huge library of books, reports, and internet pages. They draft quickly and "sound confident". They still invent details when unsure. They need a manager who briefs them, checks the work, and decides what leaves the room.

Corporate office examples:

  • HR: Draft a first reply to a candidate asking about interview timing - you still confirm the real schedule.
  • Operations: Turn a messy WhatsApp thread into a clean action list - you still assign owners.
  • Marketing: Rewrite a product post in clearer Bangla - brand/compliance still approves before publish.
  • Finance: Draft a customer note about missing loan documents - checklist still get a human check.

What AI is good at today

Strong Weak / risky
Drafting and rewriting Final hiring, legal, budget, or credit decisions
Summarizing long text Inventing “facts” when sources are missing
Turning rough notes into structure Knowing your latest internal policy unless you provide it
Brainstorming options Sending emails, publishing, or approving alone

Callout — Manager mindset
You do not need to “become a programmer.” You need to become a clear brief-giver and quality checker.

Reflection

  1. Where in your week do you already act like a manager of juniors (brief → review → approve)?
  1. What would you never let a junior send without your check?

Sec 05 · Session 0 · Opening · What is Generative AI?

1.2 What is Generative AI?

In plain words:
Generative AI creates new content from a prompt - emails, reports, Bangla/English rewrites, slide outlines, image ideas - instead of only retrieving a fixed answer from a database.

Everyday analogy:
Traditional software is like a fixed menu at a canteen: press button → get the same dish.
Generative AI is like a skilled cook: give ingredients and instructions → get a new plate each time. Taste before you serve.

Office examples

Role Generative AI can help with…
HR / Admin Offer letter, joining checklist, leave-policy FAQ from uploaded handbook
Operations Meeting notes → owners + deadlines; SOP Q&A for new joiners
Marketing / Comms Three caption options; rewrite press note; slide outline from brief
Sales / BD Visit notes → polite WhatsApp/email follow-up; proposal outline
Analyst / Strategy Clean a messy Excel narrative; “what changed vs last month?” bullets
Team lead / Manager Weekly update from scattered Slack/WhatsApp notes
Finance / Credit ops Document investment memo draft; policy summary (you verify numbers)

Sec 06 · Session 0 · Opening · AI vs Traditional Software

1.3 AI vs Traditional Software

Traditional software Generative AI
How it works Fixed rules and forms Predicts likely next words / patterns
Same input Same output every time Similar but not identical
Best for Calculations, records, workflows that must not invent Drafting, summarizing, exploring options
Risk Wrong rule → wrong result (usually consistent) Confident wrong answer (“hallucination”)
Your job Configure and operate Brief, constrain, and review

Corporate example:

  • HRIS / payroll / ERP / CRM = traditional software (leave balances, invoice totals, order status).
  • Drafting a weekly team update from messy notes = generative AI.
    Do not mix them up: AI should not “invent” a leave balance or invoice total. The system of record should.

Callout
Use traditional software for truth that must not drift. Use generative AI for language, structure, and first drafts.

Sec 10 · Section 1 · WITH · Hallucination

1.8 Hallucination

In plain words:
Hallucination is when AI invents details that sound right but are not true — fake citations, wrong numbers, made-up clause numbers, imaginary employee or customer facts.

Analogy:
A confident junior filling gaps in a board pack so it “looks complete.” Dangerous in any office where numbers and promises matter.

High-risk zones in corporate offices

  • Headcount, salary, bonus, or budget figures
  • Legal, HR, or compliance wording (“policy says…”) without a source
  • Customer or employee PII (NID, phone, salary, medical)
  • Marketing claims and competitor comparisons
  • Promises to clients, vendors, or candidates (“we will confirm by Friday”)
  • Finance-specific: rates, fees, EMI, regulatory language

Detection habits

  1. Ask: “Cite the source or say unknown.”
  2. Spot-check numbers against the system of record.
  3. Never paste secrets you should not share with a vendor tool.
  4. Prefer “draft + checklist” over “final answer.”

Trap exercise (instructor may demo)
Ask AI a question about a policy you did not upload. Note whether it invents.

What happened?

1.9 Human-in-the-loop

In plain words:
A human must approve steps that affect customers, money, legal/compliance, people decisions, or public reputation.

Red / Blue / Green (kitchen tags)

Tag Meaning Examples
🟢 Green AI thinking OK Draft, summarize, rewrite, brainstorm
🔵 Blue Rules / automation OK Forms, reminders, moving data, triggers
🔴 Red Human only Hiring, legal, conflict, budget/credit decision, send externally without review

You will reuse these tags when you map your process (Worksheet B sketch · Workflow Canvas). Same meanings every time.

Always human before…

  • [ ] External email / SMS / WhatsApp to customer, vendor, or candidate
  • [ ] Changing official records (HRIS, ERP, CRM)
  • [ ] Publishing marketing / social posts
  • [ ] HR letters with compensation or termination language
  • [ ] Hiring, budget, legal, credit, or compliance decisions

My personal red lines

e.g. (1) Any email leaving the company (2) Numbers in a board/manager pack (3) HR letters with pay or exit language

In plain words:
Context is everything AI needs in this conversation to do the job — role, product, audience, constraints, and the actual content (notes, policy excerpt, table).

Analogy:
Briefing a new hire before they write a customer letter: who the customer is, what product, what you may / may not promise.

Without context: AI guesses from the public internet.
With context: AI works from your brief.

2.3 Knowledge assistants & RAG (plain language)

Knowledge assistant: AI that answers using your documents (SOPs, product sheets, HR policy), not only public internet memory.

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) — one sentence:
Before answering, the system finds relevant passages from your files, then writes an answer grounded in those passages (ideally with citations).

Analogy:
Instead of guessing leave rules or expense policy from memory, the junior opens the HR binder on the desk, then drafts.

Good uses: leave/expense policy Q&A, onboarding FAQs, brand guidelines, product sheets your company uploaded.
Still your job: verify citations; update files when policy changes.
